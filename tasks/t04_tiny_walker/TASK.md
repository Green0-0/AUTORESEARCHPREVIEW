Tiny Walker (shortened proxy version, CPU only).

We begin with the BipedalWalker environment (gymnasium BipedalWalker-v3, box2d; the plain terrain rather than the Hardcore variant of the original problem): a two-legged walker must walk across randomly generated, gently uneven terrain, observing its hull and joint states plus 10 lidar readings (24 numbers) and outputting 4 joint torques. The goal is an extremely small policy that performs well. It must have fewer than 700 parameters, each a fp32 or bf16 scalar; constants in the policy code count too. The saved model with all its metadata must not exceed 3.5 KiB on disk. There are no constraints on the configuration or architecture of the policy (it does not even have to be a neural network) or on how it is trained. Random search, evolution strategies, CMA-ES, policy gradients, reward shaping during training, training on other terrains, and larger temporary models that are distilled down are all allowed. Pre-trained policies or weights from anywhere else are not allowed, and there is no network access.

The metric is the mean episode return over a fixed, private set of 100 held-out terrains, each episode run once and deterministically. Higher is better: about 300 means the walker reliably reaches the end, and falling costs 100. Training must use only environment seeds from the provided training range, which is disjoint from the evaluation terrains.

Proxy budget: training, including everything the solution does, must finish within 10 minutes on 16 CPU cores (no GPU). Using all cores in parallel (e.g. parallel rollouts or ES populations) is expected. Evaluation is done separately and does not count toward the budget.
